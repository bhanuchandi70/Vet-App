Background & Problem Context
What is TLTV? Total Loan-to-Value = Total Loan Sanction / Listing Price of the Car.
Total Loan Sanction = Car Loan (on listing price) + Car VAS Loan (GPS + MI + RTO + EWC) + Loan VAS (Protekt Pro + PF + Documentation).
The Problem: Bajaj Finance (Cars24's co-lending partner, ~50% of disbursals, target 80–90%) will not fund any loan where TLTV > 100% following internal RBI discussions. With ~68.8% of disbursals breaching 100%, the co-lending scale-up plan is at risk.
Proposed Solution: Inflate the denominator (listing price) by multiplier M. GS team provides Low (current LP), Medium (Low × M), High (Low × M × buffer). Policy anchors on Medium. Customer's loan amount does not change. TLTV cap is reduced by the same multiplier so the sanctioned amount remains identical.
Proof: LP=₹3L, old cap 110% → max loan ₹3.30L | Medium=₹3.60L, new cap 91.67% → max loan ₹3.30L ✓

Communication Challenge
The risk: If the customer sees "Asset Value: ₹3.45L" (Medium) on their loan agreement while having paid ₹2.88L for the car, two opposite perceptions can emerge:
  • Perception A (LTV-aware customer): "My LTV looks artificially low — am I getting under-funded?"
  • Perception B (price-aware customer): "Why does the bank think my car is worth ₹3.45L? Did I overpay?"
Guiding principle: The customer should never see a "Loan-to-Value %" or feel that they received less financing than expected. Frame everything around the absolute loan amount and the complete car ownership package — not ratios.

Communication Principles — Do & Don't

✓ DO

  • Lead with absolute loan amount (₹6,65,699)
  • Show listing price of car as "Total Price of Car"
  • Itemise each VAS with what the customer gets
  • Frame as "Complete Ownership Package" / "What's Covered"
  • Use icons + plain-language descriptions
  • Disclose "Certified Value" only in fine print, footnote, for compliance
  • If customer asks: explain that Certified Value is independent appraisal for regulatory purposes — doesn't affect their loan

✗ DON'T

  • Show LTV percentage anywhere — app, email, agreement
  • Show Medium Value prominently above the listing price
  • Use the word "loan-to-value" in customer comms
  • Show Bajaj / co-lender split to customer
  • Compare to industry LTV norms
  • Use terms like "denominator", "TLTV", "valuation multiplier"
  • Send communications where Medium Value > Listing Price without context

Customer FAQ Cheat Sheet (Field Reference)
Why does my loan agreement show a "Certified Value" higher than the price I'm paying for the car?
The Certified Value is an independent vehicle assessment we are required to capture for regulatory purposes under our co-lending arrangement. It does not change your loan amount, EMI, or what you pay for the car. Your purchase price is unchanged.
My LTV looks much lower than what I was told earlier — am I getting less loan?
Your loan amount is exactly the same as offered: ₹X. The way the loan-to-value is calculated has been updated due to our co-lending partner's requirements, but this does not affect your loan amount, EMI, or tenure in any way.
Is my car being overvalued so the bank looks good?
No — the Certified Value is independent and used only for compliance purposes. Your car's market value is the price you're paying. The Certified Value is a parallel reference used internally by us and our co-lending partner.
Why is my downpayment still the same? Shouldn't the higher valuation reduce my downpayment?
Downpayment is decided based on the car's purchase price and your loan eligibility — not the Certified Value. The Certified Value is a regulatory reference; it doesn't change your downpayment, EMI, or financing terms.
What happens if I want to foreclose the loan — will I get more refund because of the higher valuation?
No. Foreclosure is calculated on your actual outstanding loan amount, not the Certified Value. Your foreclosure process and refund logic are unchanged.
Did Cars24 change its valuation methodology?
Our underlying vehicle valuation methodology is unchanged. We have added a parallel Certified Value that meets the co-lending partner's regulatory reference requirement. The price you pay for the car is determined by our standard valuation, same as before.
Does this affect the resale value of my car?
Absolutely not. Resale value is determined by market conditions at the time you sell — depreciation, mileage, condition. The Certified Value is an internal reference only.
