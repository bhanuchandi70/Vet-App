TLTV Reduction Analysis — Cars24 NBFC

Problem: Bajaj co-lending requires TLTV ≤ 100% | Dataset: Internal Disbursals Only
📅 Jan – Apr 2026 📊 7,898 Loans Analysed 🏦 Internal Disbursals 📋 Policy: RBv6 🔢 DS1 Max Values Used
Background & Problem Context
What is TLTV? Total Loan-to-Value = Total Loan Sanction / Listing Price of the Car.
Total Loan Sanction = Car Loan (on listing price) + Car VAS Loan (GPS + MI + RTO + EWC) + Loan VAS (Protekt Pro + PF + Documentation).
Because VAS amounts sit in the numerator but the denominator is only the listing price, TLTV structurally exceeds 100% for most loans.
The Problem: Bajaj Finance — Cars24's co-lending partner (currently ~50% of disbursals, target 80–90%) — has decided it will not fund any loan where TLTV > 100% following internal RBI discussions. With ~68.8% of disbursals currently breaching 100% TLTV, the co-lending scale-up plan is at risk.
Proposed Solution (Denominator Engineering): Inflate the denominator (listing price) by a multiplier M. The GS (car sales) team provides three asset values in the valuation report — Low (current listing price), Medium (Low × M), High (Low × M × buffer). Policy anchors on Medium for underwriting. The customer's loan amount does not change. TLTV cap is reduced by the same multiplier so the sanctioned amount remains identical.

Example: Listing Price = ₹3,00,000 | Current TLTV cap = 110% → Max loan = ₹3,30,000 | Medium value (M=1.20) = ₹3,60,000 | New TLTV cap = 110%/1.20 = 91.67% | New max loan = 91.67% × ₹3,60,000 = ₹3,30,000 ✓ (unchanged)

M = Listing Price multiplier such that: TLTV_new = TLS / (Listing Price × M) ≤ 100% For each loan: minimum M needed = its current TLTV (= TLS / Listing Price)

Part 2 — ASP Bucket-Level Analysis
Methodology: Loans are split into 6 ASP buckets: <₹3L, ₹3–4.5L, ₹4.5–6L, ₹6–7.5L, ₹7.5–9L, ₹9L+. For each bucket:
  1. Compute the median ASP of all loans in that bucket (anchor for VAS %)
  2. For each VAS component: take Max DS1 value within bucket (P95 if Max > 2×P95)
  3. Express each VAS value as % of that bucket's median ASP → individual VAS %s
  4. Sum → Total Car VAS % and Total Loan VAS % for that bucket
  5. Apply these %s uniformly to each loan's listing price as denominator expansion (Step 2, 3)
  6. Measure % of loans still >100% TLTV at each step

This approach standardises VAS impact within each bucket and is the basis for the per-bucket multiplier.

Why the gap between 1.349x and 1.195x? The VAS-implied multiplier (1.349x) is derived from the theoretical maximum of each VAS funded simultaneously at their DS1 ceiling. In practice, no single loan has all VAS at their maximum — the actual TLTV never exceeds 1.277x in the data. The empirical P99 of 1.195x is therefore the more grounded number. The difference (~15%) is the "unused" VAS headroom.

Open Points & Actions Required
⚠ Open Point 1 — The <3L Bucket Tension
VAS-implied M for <3L is 1.349x, significantly higher than the recommended consolidated M of 1.20x. However, the actual TLTV P99 in this bucket is only 1.194x and the absolute max is 1.277x — confirming that all-VAS-at-max doesn't occur in practice.

Recommendation: Apply the consolidated 1.20x multiplier AND introduce a policy cap on Car VAS funding for ASP <₹3L (particularly Motor Insurance, which hits 10.1% of median ASP). The ~15 loans/month in the residual 1% can be managed via manual review or by excluding sub-₹3L loans from co-lending where TLTV remains elevated.
⚠ Open Point 2 — Motor Insurance Outliers
MI max is consistently 2x–6x the P95 across buckets (e.g., ₹74,745 max vs ₹24,109 P95 in aggregate). These outliers are being capped at P95 for the multiplier calculation. Investigate these high-MI cases — are they data errors, or genuinely high-value insurance for modified/premium variants? If systematic, consider a hard cap on MI funding in the DS1 model.
⚠ Open Point 3 — RTO Outliers (Consistent Pattern)
RTO max exceeds 2×P95 in 5 of 6 buckets and is capped at P95 throughout. Aggregate max is ₹84,725 vs P95 of ₹19,042 — a 4.4× gap. This is a consistent pattern suggesting certain states (likely Maharashtra, Kerala) have very high RTO fees. Consider a geographic cap or separate multiplier flag for high-RTO states.
⚠ Open Point 4 — Customer Communication
Once the multiplier is applied, all customer-facing documents (TNC acceptance email, sanction letter, loan agreement) must be updated to remove any reference to: listing price, asset value, TLTV, or LTV percentage. Only show: total loan sanction, EMI, tenure, rate of interest. Showing the "Medium" asset value to the customer would create confusion since it exceeds the price they paid.
⚠ Open Point 5 — Weighted vs. Max Consolidated Multiplier Decision
There are 4 possible consolidated multiplier choices ranging from 1.187x to 1.349x. The recommended 1.20x is a rounded version of the data-driven max (1.195x). If the business wants to future-proof against VAS expansion, the weighted VAS-implied M of 1.269x (≈1.27x) may be preferred. Decision should involve the Risk, Finance and Compliance teams jointly.
